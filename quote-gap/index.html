Conflicts and gaps

Premium order is A, then C, then B. That order hides a $6.2M building hole on A and a 5% wind deductible on B.

  • A writes $42.0M on a $48.2M building. A total loss leaves $6.2M uninsured.
  • A is named peril. B and C are special form. Water damage, collapse, and most theft sit outside A.
  • Wind and hail on building plus BPP ($57.6M). A and C take 2% ($1.15M). B takes 5% ($2.88M). Same storm, $1.73M more out of pocket on B.
  • Flood. Only B offers a limit, and it is a $2.5M sublimit on a Bayport warehouse. A and C exclude it.
  • Ordinance or law. A excludes it. C pays Coverage A only. B gives A plus B at $1.0M. 1987 tilt-wall rebuild will hit current Houston fire and wind code.
  • Business interruption. A is $12.0M for 12 months. That is $4.8M under the $16.8M TIV. B is $18.0M for 18 months. C matches TIV for 12 months.
  • Soft costs appear only on B ($250k). Cyber appears only on B ($1.0M first party). Quake appears only on C ($5.0M).

Talk-track

Open the compare, not the premiums. A is cheapest because it is $6.2M short on the building and named peril. Raise the holes that survive a Houston wind or flood year. Leave Harris County quake for the lender if they asked.

  • Ask whether they will sit with a $6.2M uninsured building on A, or pay C to match TIV, or buy B’s surplus limit.
  • Read the wind number in dollars. B’s 5% is about $1.73M more than A or C on the same storm.
  • Do not treat B’s $2.5M flood sublimit as a flood program. Park NFIP plus excess flood as a separate bind.
  • On a 1987 tilt-wall, ask whether a total-loss rebuild clears current Houston fire and wind code without ordinance Coverage B and C.
  • A’s BI is $4.8M light. If the WMS is down more than a year, A is the wrong form even if the building limit were fixed.
  • B’s $1.0M cyber is a bolt-on, not a tech program. If the warehouse management system is material, place cyber on its own.
